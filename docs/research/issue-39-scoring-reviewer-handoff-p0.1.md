# Invitation: independent scoring reviewer for a synthetic privacy benchmark

**PUBLIC INVITATION ONLY — not an approved score, protocol or privacy rule.** You do not need to know Hylja. It is an early research project testing whether private details can be hidden before an AI model used by an engineering assistant receives them, while enough task detail remains useful. Its current examples are made up and run in a local test setup, **not** a deployed privacy safeguard.

## What you would review

The *reference candidate* is the experimental program being tested. Other people must independently decide **what information may go to a particular model** (policy reviewer) and **what counts as a correct engineering answer** (task reviewer). Your separate role would be to define **how the results are counted and compared**, without choosing treatments or fixing the answer key to match or oppose the candidate's behavior. Public practice tasks D01/D02/D05 exist; H01–H04 name only future hidden-case categories, not stored cases. A separate human custodian would create and guard future hidden cases and answers; volunteering for scoring does not appoint you to custody or test ownership. You need no hidden contents or answers to volunteer or review proposed public scoring rules.

**Now:** Please only say whether you are willing and disclose conflicts—no final metrics, score or sign-off yet. **If later appointed**, you would help decide how to count missed private details, harmless details hidden by mistake, forbidden information actually sent, tasks solved and cases we could not test. The detailed questions would include:

- How to count deliberately inserted *synthetic* private details detected or missed, false alarms (harmless details incorrectly flagged) and over-hiding separately for names, email and phone. Define a one-to-one match to an independently planted example by field and location. Today's public examples mark Unicode code-point positions inside each decoded text field, **not** positions in the serialized JSON file; agree how those source positions map through later input normalization to candidate-reported UTF-8 byte positions before scoring, with rules for overlaps and repeated values. If no eligible examples exist, report **not applicable (`n/a`)**, not a fabricated zero. A later judge or safety check stopping a leak does **not** erase a detection miss.
- How to distinguish **attempted leaks**, forbidden bytes actually delivered to the controlled test destination in the final message body or accompanying metadata (including partial or encoded secret forms and repeated sends), and attempted sends whose destination was not reliably observed. A separately verified zero-byte block is an observed no-send for that attempt but leaves the engineering task unsolved; an attempted send without reliable sink monitoring is **untested**, not proof of no leak.
- Which failures are **hard gates** (a failure no average score can offset, such as a forbidden synthetic secret reaching a disallowed test destination), versus measurements of task correctness, valid output, over-hiding, abstention (the candidate declined to decide), coverage, time and cost. Decide weights—or explicitly compare without weights—**before** viewing a scored candidate result. Untested and unsupported cases are not passes; cases outside the evaluated system's architecture are explicitly outside scope, not passes.

These are **questions, not accepted metrics or policy**. The [public rubric](issue-39-public-rubric-p0.1.md) is a draft. [#65 information-category research](https://github.com/Marcus-Levin/hylja/issues/65) and [#66 separation of privacy, sensitivity, trust and task-detail needs](https://github.com/Marcus-Levin/hylja/issues/66) must settle enough vocabulary for [#39](https://github.com/Marcus-Levin/hylja/issues/39)'s answer key **before any final scoring sign-off or v0 freeze**. We can discuss public questions now. The already exposed reference program was built before rules were agreed and is practice-only. For a fair score, the team must approve and freeze the rules **before building a fresh eligible candidate** (the prospective pre-tuning lock), with restricted blind custody; a later final freeze pins the exact scorer and test setup before a blind run. Do not change the answer key or metrics to help or penalize a candidate after seeing its outcome.

## An easy first reply — only willingness, not approval

> I am willing / not willing to discuss the independent scoring-reviewer role. I have / have not developed, directed or tuned the reference candidate; any exposure to its public results is ____. I have / have not seen any hidden case content or answers (none should exist yet). I also hold / do not hold another proposed review or blind-custody role (which one, without names or contact details): ____. I can review public scoring questions now, but this reply **does not approve** metrics, policy, task answers or a benchmark freeze. My questions are ____.

A person taking two roles needs a recorded conflict review and an independent cross-check; willingness does not automatically fill missing roles. Ask the project owner for an **approved private channel** before sharing your identity, contact or custody details. Later actual approval needs a restricted record of reviewer identity, exact revised scorer/rubric version, match/denominator/`n/a` rules, leakage and task/failure gates, weighting, rationale, objections and date. A separate protocol owner records the distinct policy, task, scoring, blind and chronology sign-offs; no reviewer may approve all of them alone. Do not send hidden cases, answers, seeds, storage locations, keys, sensitive digests, real customer/employee/credential information or reviewer contact details in this chat, Git, pull requests or automated CI. If you ever access blind contents or answers, do not develop, tune or direct the candidate while those cases remain eligible.

The [open-gates packet](issue-39-v0-open-gates-p0.1.md) lists unresolved decisions; only [the plan](../plan.md#current-state) records implementation status. This invitation gives no authority to send data to a model, publish a blind score, select a product, restore originals or use real data.
